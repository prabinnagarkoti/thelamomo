1. Project Background  
1.1 Problem Background 
The rapid advancement of digital technology has essentially changed the way 
consumers avail food delivery services worldwide. According to Huang and Siao (2023), 
online food delivery platforms have gained immense popularity worldwide, and this has been 
stimulated by the digital transformation. These platforms, however, combine intense financial 
and operational burdens on the small independent food businesses directly. The Commission 
provided by third-party platforms is 20-25% and 30-35% on a per-order basis (Chan et al., 
2023). As Sharffudin and Yadav (2025) point out, the profit margins are constantly depleted 
by commission fees, and thus, businesses cannot build direct customer loyalty programmes.  
1.2 Project Objectives 
BizMenu Builder will be launched as a minimum viable product at the end of the 
twelve-week capstone timeframe. The site will allow entrepreneurs to have branded profiles 
with custom logos and colour schemes. Live menu management allows the owners to do real-
time updates to the prices of items, item descriptions, and availability. Customers are able to 
browse the menu, search, and place orders using a branded storefront on a commission-free 
basis. Order notification to the owner is delivered instantly, and full ownership of customer 
data eradicates the reliance on third parties. 
1.3 Stakeholders and Significance 
Small food business owners are the primary stakeholders because they obtain 
financial independence and control over the customer data. There are secondary stakeholders 
such as customers who will have direct advantage of a smooth, personally branded, and 
commission free ordering process. The project is directly targeting the problem of digital 
inequity that is dealing with small-to-medium food businesses operating in the competitive 
Australian food service industry. 
2. Literature Review  
2.1 Analysis of Existing Solutions, Technologies, and Methods 
 
The online food delivery market in the world has grown at an impressive rate in the 
last ten years. According to Fu et al. (2024), the services of online meal delivery now create 
significant social and sustainability concerns on communities in the global context. The Uber 
Eats platform and DoorDash are multi-sided digital marketplaces that unite customers, 
restaurants and couriers in one place. This powerful market place paradigm, though, always 
subject small independent food enterprises worldwide to financial and operational drawbacks. 
2.2 Existing Solutions 
Online food delivery platforms are driven by profit-seeking behaviour that, as claimed 
by Dai and Wu (2023), impacts the profitability of the small restaurant enterprises in a 
harmful way. Such platforms create unrealistic demands of commissions and leave 
2 
 
independent restaurants with no choice but trade-offs of either charging more or less, service 
and operations. White label substitutes, such as GloriaFood, ChowNow and UpMenu, enable 
restaurants to roll out uniquely labeled order websites on a month-to-month subscription 
framework. In this context, Ocloo et al. (2024) suggest that owner IT skills play a critical role 
in the digitisation of business performance of restaurant SMEs. This technical obstacle makes 
most of the currently existing white-label platforms inaccessible to operators who lack special 
IT knowledge or assets. These platforms are also shared multi-tenant, and not a full-fledged 
private per-business isolated environment. 
2.3 Technologies Selected and Justification                                                                                     
In an attempt to meet the identified access and usability limitations, BizMenu Builder 
implements a current full-stack JavaScript design based on Next.js, MongoDB, and Tailwind 
CSS. Next.js has been selected as the main framework since it combines both the frontend 
React elements and the backend API routes into a single codebase, which also means that 
there will be no additional Express server and makes the deployment considerably easier 
(Subramanian, 2019). The Next.js App Router architecture also supports server-side 
rendering and static generation, which is better optimised towards performance and search 
engine optimisation of the customer-facing storefront. Mongoose is the Object Data 
Modelling (ODM) library and MongoDB Atlas is the cloud-based NoSQL database that are 
implemented to impose schema checking and handle structured data operations (Banker et al., 
2016). MongoDB supports dynamic menu structures and order records with document-based 
model, without define relational schema. The responsiveness of the interface and reliable way 
of processing orders is of paramount importance in customer satisfaction in food ordering 
apps, which Ahmad et al. (2024) emphasize. This finding is the main rationale why Next.js 
and MongoDB Atlas have been selected in the given project. Vercel is the deployment 
platform since it is the native hosting solution of Next.js, with zero-configuration 
deployment, built-in CI/CD integration with GitHub, and serverless edge computing which 
makes small food operators spend less on infrastructure (Lehmann and Recker, 
2022).                                                                                                                                                                     
4 Gap Identified 
There is still no solution available that is free-to-prototype and includes zero commissions, 
complete information isolation, and real-time menu switching in a single white-label solution. 
Alternatives that do exist are priced by subscription or require technical configuration, which 
the small independent operators are not able to meet. Most solutions are not entirely private 
but more of multi-tenant marketplaces, having their own brands in each business case. 
BizMenu Builder fills this gap by offering a lightweight and cloud-based solution that is 
specifically designed to support individual small food businesses. Each deployment runs

separately, which guarantees the full ownership of the data, control of the brand, and free 
transactions with the customers without commissions. 
3. Design 
3.1 Architecture Diagram 
 
Figure 3.1.1: Architecture Diagram                 
 BizMenu Builder is a modern full-stack architecture that is scalable. Customers/ Restaurant 
Owners communicate with the application using either a web or mobile browser via HTTPS. 
The UI, cart system, and authentication are located in the Frontend Layer and manage 
application state with React Context and are created with Next.js and deployed on Vercel. 
Next.js API Routes are used in the Backend Layer to perform business logic, request validation, 
and serverless functions, communicating via REST/JSON (Subramanian, 2019). Although our 
original plan was Render, we transferred to Vercel to facilitate our Next.js framework. Being 
the original home of Next.js, Vercel has zero-configuration deployment, better serverless edge 
performance, and native CI/CD integration with GitHub, which will eventually lower the 
amount of DevOps overhead on our part. 
3.2 Database Model 
4 
 
BizMenu Builder database model is built based on NoSQL with MongoDB Atlas to support 
flexible and scalable food menu data. Mongoose is another Object Data Modelling (ODM) 
tool that the system uses to organize and manipulate data. 
MenuItem Collection 
The basic model of the database is the MenuItem, into which all food-related information 
(e.g., item name, description, price, and category) is inserted. This flexible structure allows 
for easy updating and addition of fields in a non-relational manner. 
Schema Flexibility 
The document-based format provided by MongoDB can easily update menu items, which 
allows restaurant proprietors to update the menu in real-time without experiencing a drop in 
system performance. 
Data Validation (Mongoose) 
Mongoose also takes care to ensure that all the data being entered into the system is in a 
specific format, thereby reducing the chances of error and ensuring that all data is in a 
specific format. 
Scalability 
The NoSQL system also allows for horizontal scaling, thereby enabling the system to cope 
with more and more users as the system increases in scope (Dalal et al. 2023). 
 
 
5 
 
Future Extensions 
 
The database currently includes four core collections: MenuItem, Order, User, and 
RestaurantConfig. Future iterations may extend the schema to include additional collections 
such as Reviews, Payments, and Notifications to support expanded analytics and customer 
engagement features. 
Cloud Integration 
 
MongoDB Atlas offers a safe and cloud-based data platform, which does not require 
local infrastructure and is highly available.

2016).3.3 Activity Diagram 
 
Figure 3.3.1: Activity Diagram 
 
This diagram displays the BizMenu Builder system flow of activities, interface 
between the user and frontend, backend and database. The operation starts with the user 
browsing or searching contents in the menu using the interface. This is forwarded to the 
backend, where validation of inputs is done. In the event that this is valid, the query is 
handled by the system and menu data is retrieved from the MongoDB database. The findings 
are then presented to the user in the form of a grid in an orderly manner. The client is able to 
choose articles and put them in the cart, and make an order. The order is verified based on a 
backend. The order is saved to the database, and a confirmation is generated in case the order 
is valid. Messages are shown in case of invalid input or errors in orders.

3.4 Wireframes 
The BizMenu Builder application has been developed by creating wireframes to 
illustrate the structure and user interface of this application in advance. The design is guided 
by these wireframes, which made it user-friendly to the customer and the restaurant owners. 
The primary customer interface wireframe consists of a clean homepage with highlighting 
the “Today’s Selection” section, where featured dishes are presented in a card-based 
layout. Each card displays a food image, item name, short description, price, and an Add 
button to place orders easily. The interface also includes a live availability indicator, 
showing which items are currently available to order. This layout simplifies browsing and 
ensures a smooth ordering experience across different devices. 
 
Figure 3.4.1: Wireframes         
The admin interface wireframe provides a clear and functional dashboard for restaurant 
owners. It supports essential features such as adding, editing, and deleting menu items, as 
well as managing orders efficiently. Navigation components, including the header, menu 
options, and action buttons, are strategically positioned to promote usability. The wireframe 
design follows a mobile-first approach, ensuring responsiveness and accessibility on 
smartphones, tablets, and desktop computers.

and are in the project (Lehmann & Recker, 2022). Database connectivity errors are another 
issue that is solved by entering environment variables correctly and providing secure 
MongoDB Atlas connection strings.
